Part 3: Your Own Searches, step by step

What Claude does during Part 3 of the retrieval activity, how it does each step, and why the step is there. In Part 3 the student describes movies they know in their own words, without the title, and sees whether TF-IDF finds them.

Time: about 7 minutesStarts after: commit Part 2 doneEnds with: commit Part 3 done
Claude acts Student decides
  1. 1

    Ask for a question

    Student decides
    How

    Asks for a question: a movie they know, described in their own words without its title. If they ask what kinds to try, Claude gives only the README's three: "a detail, the plot retold, something vague".

    Why

    The questions are the student's. Claude never suggests, completes, rewords or fixes the spelling of one, so the results reflect how a real person would ask.

  2. 2

    Run search.py on it

    Claude actsRepeats for 3 questions
    How

    Runs uv run python search.py '<their question>' exactly as typed, quoted so the shell passes it through unchanged. TF-IDF is the default method.

    Why

    It ranks all 4,309 movies by how many weighted words their plot summaries share with the question, using the Part 1 settings.

    Inside search.py

    What the script does in one run, in order. It uses code from retrieval.py and scikit-learn.

    1. a
      Clean up the question. Collapses extra spaces and prints it back (the first 25 words). An empty question stops here. The student sees exactly what was searched.
    2. b
      Build the TF-IDF matrix. Loads the 4,309 plot summaries and the settings in tfidf_settings.py, then builds one row of word weights per movie, as in Part 2. The question is compared against the same model the student configured.
    3. c
      Turn the question into a row. vectorizer.transform weights the question's words with the same vocabulary. Stop words (if removed) and words no summary uses drop out. The question and the movies have to be lists of numbers over the same words to be compared.
    4. d
      Score every movie. The cosine similarity of the question's row with each movie's row: how closely the two point the same way. The rows already have length 1, so this is a dot product. One score from 0 (no word in common) to 1 (most alike) puts all movies on one scale.
    5. e
      Rank and cut. Sorts the scores high to low, drops movies that scored 0, and keeps the top 10. Ties keep the order of data/movies.jsonl. Movies with no shared word have no meaningful order, so they aren't listed.
    6. f
      List the matched words. For each result, the question's words that are also in that summary, the one that adds most to the score first. The student can see why each movie came up.
    7. g
      List the ignored words. Prints which of the question's words counted for nothing: stop words, or words not in the vocabulary. It shows what the settings threw away from the question.
  3. 3

    Paste the output in full

    Claude acts
    How

    Copies the output exactly into a code block, then asks for the next question until there are three. Claude does not say which results are right or where their movie landed.

    Why

    The student's terminal hides tool output. Judging the results is their job, not Claude's.

  4. 4

    Record the questions

    Claude acts
    How

    Writes the three questions into the slot "Your questions, as you typed them" in WRITEUP.md, one per line, word for word, typos included.

    Why

    The writeup keeps a record of exactly what was searched, so it can be compared to the results.

  5. 5

    Ask what TF-IDF got right and wrong

    Student decides
    How

    Asks the slot's question: did the movies you meant come up, and what did TF-IDF get right and wrong? A sentence or two is enough.

    Why

    Reading the matched and ignored words against their own question is how they learn what keyword search can and can't do.

  6. 6

    Write their answer into WRITEUP.md

    Claude acts
    How

    Replaces the slot's XXXX with their words exactly and shows the slot as it now reads. Tidying is offered as a question, never done silently.

    Why

    The writeup is graded on the student's own words.

  7. 7

    Save the part to GitHub

    Claude acts
    How

    Runs python3 dump_transcript.py, stages WRITEUP.md and tfidf_settings.py by name, commits Part 3 done and pushes. A hook blocks the commit if a Part 3 slot still reads XXXX.

    Why

    Progress is saved even if class ends, and the commit marks where the next session starts (Part 4).

Rules that hold across the part

  • Claude never gives an example question or changes one.
  • Claude never says a result looks good or right, and gives no caveat in Part 3.
  • Explaining TF-IDF, the score, or the matched words is always allowed when the student asks.
  • Three questions is enough in class; more are welcome at home.
