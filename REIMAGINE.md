# Problem

When completing activities or homework with Claude, it is very difficult to keep track of the steps Claude runs or completes without assistance for intermediary steps. Often, it feels like being blindly led and having to trust the process of answering the questions. 

# Evidence

From the survey responses we notice that a total of 8 out of 12 people did not disagree with the statement”Sometimes I said yes to Claude without really understanding what I was agreeing to”. Suggesting that us as students may prioritize deadline and completion rather than the whole process of the assignment, as we entrust that Claude is leading us to the correct solutions and process. Particularly of the 8 who did not disagree, 5 stated they agreed, confirming the unsureness of understanding Claude’s process. Another statement of evidence is that 9 out of 12 students were neutral to the statement “I could explain my results to a classmate without Claude’s help”. We find this interesting because I think some portion of the assignments could be explained but the overall flow or explanation process is disrupted by not fully grasping the steps Claude took versus us as students.

# Solutions

First: While Claude is running the code for intermediary steps, it would give us a short visual flow chart or brief steps process of what it is doing, how it is accomplishing these steps, and the purpose of each step. The benefit of this solution would be providing a visual representation of Claude’s contribution to the project which one could reflect on or compare in the end. The cons is that were may be many intermediary steps for an assignment and this could become tedious, or increase completion of the assignment tremendously. 

Second: While Claude is running the code for intermediary steps, it would have several checkpoints to check students' understanding of the steps it is doing. These checkpoints might be asking concept questions or a summary of what it just did. The pros of this solution is that it targets the problem of answering yes without thinking as well as not keeping track of what Claude is doing. The cons is that there might be too many checkpoints along the way.

Third: One solution could be letting students use Claude in their own way. Students might use it to code, debug or rewrite. The pros is students would be well aware of what they are using Claude for. The cons would be that the amount of time to finish the assignment would be tremendous and students might have used the wrong implementation which might lead to misunderstanding of the concepts.

# Prototype

We asked Claude to implement the first solution, it gave us a dashboard with an overview of the steps for Part 2 and Part 3. The first attempt at creating a visual representation Claude provided a good structure of steps that Claude versus the student was implementing and why. It lacked a clearer depth of steps it ran itself, so we adjusted the model to include specific steps of what Claude ran, for part 2 and part 3. Overall successful in helping understand the flow of Claude’s contribution to the assignment, and it would be helpful for future assignments to ask for a similar visual of the process.
